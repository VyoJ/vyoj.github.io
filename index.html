Vyoman Jain

Incoming Master of Science in Technology and Design (AI) student at Singapore University of Technology and Design.

Previously at Apple, CognitiveLab, and Sarvam AI. CSE undergrad from PES University, Bangalore.

I’m interested in computer vision and building systems for AI. My work so far has been vision-language models for documents in languages the field has left behind, and the data and inference infrastructure they depend on.

I helped build Nayana at CognitiveLab: a family of document-centric vision-language models trained on synthetic data. That meant producing over 2 million pages across 22 languages, then using it to train Nayana-OCR and the NetraEmbed retrieval models. Nayana won Meta’s Llama Impact Grant in 2025, one of ten globally.

Alongside that I’ve worked on document parsing pipelines at Sarvam AI, synthetic data for robot manipulation at IIIT Bangalore, porting llama.cpp to IBM z/OS mainframes, and AI-assisted issue triage at Apple. My capstone, HyperCluster, shards LLMs across a self-organizing peer-to-peer cluster of edge devices.

I also worked on manimator, which turns research papers into animated explanations (featured in Hugging Face’s Spaces of the Week). Outside of work, I organize quantum computing events as a Qiskit Advocate, and I’ve taught RAG and LLM agents to undergraduates through PESU I/O.

I’m always looking for new research opportunties, so if any of this overlaps with what you’re looking for, please do reach out!

selected publications

  1. DAIS
    HyperCluster: Decentralized Large Language Model Inference over Peer-to-Peer Wireless Networks
    P. Samarth, Vyoman Jain, Sanjiv Raghunandan, and 2 more authors
    In IFIP International Conference on Distributed Applications and Interoperable Systems (DAIS). Also presented as a poster at the ML4Wireless workshop, AAAI 2026 (non-archival), 2026
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Large language models’ substantial memory and compute requirements hinder deployment on individual resource-constrained devices. HyperCluster is a framework for fully decentralized collaborative inference over wireless networks, contributing a ring-based pipelined inference protocol in which nodes deterministically self-organize into a computational ring by device capability and pass intermediate states directly between peers, and a generalizable model sharding methodology built on HuggingFace Transformers that automatically partitions any dense LLM across heterogeneous devices according to available compute and memory.

  2. ICML Workshop
    ReviewArena: A Large-Scale Cross-Conference Dataset and Benchmark for LLM Peer Review
    P. Samarth, Motamarri Sai Sathvik, Vyoman Jain, and 1 more author
    In ICML 2026 Workshop on AI for Science, 2026
    Spotlight Abs Bib HTML

    Spotlight paper

    ReviewArena is a large-scale peer-review dataset constructed from OpenReview venues (NeurIPS, ICLR, ICML, CoRL, COLM, EMNLP, TMLR) comprising 51,529 papers and 196,099 reviews across fourteen review fields, including full PDFs, reviewer scores and text, rebuttals, meta-reviews, and final decisions. We further derive ReviewArena-Eval, a 1,002-paper benchmark spanning six conferences with aligned, venue-specific evaluation protocols. Baselines with six open-weight LLMs show that current models are miscalibrated, compress rating scales, and weakly distinguish accepted from rejected papers.

  3. AAAI Workshop
    M3DR: Towards Universal Multilingual Multimodal Document Retrieval
    Adithya S. Kolavi and Vyoman Jain
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2512.03514. Accepted at the AAAI 2026 workshop on New Frontiers in Information Retrieval (non-archival), 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML

    Existing document retrieval systems are overwhelmingly English-centric. M3DR uses synthetic multilingual document data and generalizes across vision-language architectures to enable semantic search across languages, employing contrastive training to learn unified representations for text and document images. Evaluated on 22 typologically diverse languages across monolingual, multilingual, and mixed-language settings, the resulting NetraEmbed and ColNetraEmbed models achieve state-of-the-art performance with roughly 150% relative improvements on cross-lingual retrieval, supporting both dense-vector and token-level multi-vector retrieval.

  4. ICCV Workshop
    Nayana: A Foundation for Document-Centric Vision-Language Models via Multi-Task, Multimodal, and Multilingual Data Synthesis
    Adithya S. Kolavi and Vyoman Jain
    In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF International Conference on Computer Vision (ICCV) Workshops — Computer Vision for Developing Countries. Also presented as a poster at the CVPR 2025 Workshop on Vision Language Models for All (non-archival), 2025
    Abs Bib HTML

    Nayana is a synthetically generated dataset for advancing document-centric vision-language models across tasks and languages. It comprises three interconnected subsets: a base dataset of 3 million document images with hierarchical annotations covering layout, textual content, reading order, and element relationships; a multilingual variant spanning 22 languages that preserves original layout while translating text through contextual models; and an information-retrieval subset of roughly 250,000 image-query pairs per language. Together these enable training for layout detection, equation recognition, captioning, markdown conversion, multilingual OCR, and document retrieval.

  5. NAACL Workshop
    Nayana OCR: A Scalable Framework for Document OCR in Low-Resource Languages
    Adithya S. Kolavi, P. Samarth, and Vyoman Jain
    In Proceedings of the 1st Workshop on Language Models for Underserved Communities (LM4UC), NAACL 2025, 2025
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    Modern vision-language models remain constrained by the scarcity of training data in non-English languages. Nayana is a scalable framework for adapting VLMs to low-resource languages through a layout-aware synthetic data generation pipeline combined with parameter-efficient adaptation, enabling existing models to learn new languages from purely synthetic data. Using LoRA, the framework demonstrates capability across ten Indic languages: Bengali, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Tamil, and Telugu.

  6. ICML Workshop
    Manimator: Transforming Research Papers into Visual Explanations
    P. Samarth, Vyoman Jain, Shiva Golugula, and 1 more author
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2507.14306. Accepted at the NewInML workshop, ICML 2025 (poster, non-archival), 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML Code

    Understanding complex scientific concepts in dense research papers is a significant challenge. Manimator uses large language models to transform research papers and natural language prompts into explanatory animations via the Manim engine: an LLM interprets the input text or PDF to produce a structured scene description outlining key concepts, formulas, and visual elements, and a second code-focused LLM translates that description into executable Manim Python code. The open-source system serves as an educational tool for rapidly creating visual explanations of STEM topics.
